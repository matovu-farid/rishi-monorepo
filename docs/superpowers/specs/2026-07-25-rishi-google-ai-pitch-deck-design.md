# Rishi Google AI Lab Pitch Deck — Design

## Objective

Create a concise, launch-stage pitch deck for Rishi’s Google AI Lab application. The deck should explain the product clearly, show credible technical/product progress, and avoid inventing traction, revenue, market size, or user-validation metrics.

## Audience and use

- Audience: Google AI Lab reviewers.
- Use: application attachment/link; optimized for asynchronous reading.
- Format: Google Slides, approximately 10 slides, shareable as viewer link.
- Stage framing: Rishi is launch-ready and approaching or entering its first App Store release. Exact App Store status/date remains an editable field.

## Visual direction

“Calm reading intelligence”: deep navy, warm paper/cream, restrained blue accents, editorial typography, generous whitespace, and product screenshots where available. The visual language should make Rishi feel trustworthy and thoughtful rather than noisy or speculative.

## Story arc

1. Cover: Rishi — Make every page more understandable.
2. Problem: long, dense documents are difficult to read, search, remember, and discuss.
3. Product: an AI-powered reading companion for books and documents.
4. Voice: read aloud and voice conversation extend reading beyond the screen.
5. AI layer: contextual chat, document text, retrieval, semantic/vector search, and book-specific conversations.
6. Workflow: library → reader → highlights/bookmarks → search → conversation, with sync across devices.
7. Launch stage: first App Store release; product capabilities are built, while real-world measurement begins at launch.
8. Launch measurement: activation, books imported, reading/listening sessions, AI questions, retention, and qualitative feedback.
9. Why Rishi / why now: founder perspective plus the opportunity created by conversational AI.
10. Ask: AI product guidance, model/evaluation expertise, and launch mentorship from Google AI Lab.

## Evidence boundaries

Supported by repository evidence:

- PDF and EPUB reading flows; MOBI-related format support exists in the desktop code.
- Read-aloud/TTS and voice-chat features are represented in product code and design documents.
- Text search, vector search, embeddings/indexing, and book-specific conversations are represented in the Electron preload contract and product designs.
- Highlights, bookmarks, sync, authentication, billing/trial flows, and cross-platform work are represented in recent implementation/design history.

Must remain placeholders until supplied by the founder:

- App Store URL and exact release status/date.
- Founder/team biography and location.
- User count, usage, retention, revenue, pilots, testimonials, or other validation metrics.
- Market size and competitor comparison.
- Funding/support request specifics.

The deck must explicitly say Rishi is pre-revenue and does not yet have meaningful usage/validation metrics if those facts remain current.

## First-draft acceptance criteria

- A reviewer can understand Rishi’s product and intended user within the first three slides.
- Every factual product claim can be traced to repository evidence or a founder-supplied placeholder.
- No fabricated traction or market numbers appear.
- The deck has a clear launch-stage narrative and a specific Google AI Lab ask.
- The resulting Slides document is editable and shareable with viewer access.

## Adversarial review

### Research review

- Critical/high issue found: repository evidence does not establish market size, revenue, user traction, or founder biography. Resolution: keep these as explicit placeholders.
- High issue found: launch timing is uncertain. Resolution: use “launching its first version on the App Store” plus an editable exact-status field.

### Design review

- Critical/high issue found: none after revision. Product, launch stage, and evidence boundaries are separated.
- Note: avoid presenting technical implementation details as proof of user value; pair each capability with the user benefit.

### Implementation review target

Before sharing the final link, verify slide text, links, placeholders, and viewer permissions. Do not claim the deck is final until those checks pass.
