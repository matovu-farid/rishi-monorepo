# Rishi Reader Social Features — Rules and Terms Draft

**Status:** Product/legal-review draft; not final legal advice
**Applies to:** Reading Groups, Reading Cycles, and Reading Sessions

This draft describes how Rishi’s reader-social features are intended to work. It must be reviewed and adapted by qualified counsel before publication, especially for age, copyright, privacy, intermediary liability, and consumer-protection requirements in each launch jurisdiction.

## 1. What the feature is

Rishi provides small, reader-focused spaces where people can read the same book together. A Reading Group can contain more than five members, but each live Reading Session is limited to five participants. Groups may move from one book to another over time.

Rishi is not a public ebook catalogue, public file-sharing directory, or general-purpose social network.

## 2. Membership and visibility

- Public groups may appear in search, but joining always requires administrator approval.
- Private groups are available through an exact identifier or invitation.
- Before approval, a requester sees only the group’s name, current book, schedule, and member count.
- After approval, members may see other members’ usernames and limited profiles within that group.
- Users may not use Rishi to scrape, collect, publish, or redistribute member identities.
- Rishi does not provide global people search, follows, or unsolicited direct messaging.

## 3. Organizer responsibilities

Organizers must use the controls responsibly. They may approve or reject members, choose books already in their Rishi library, organize sessions, transfer control, and remove members. They must not use a group to harass, discriminate unlawfully, distribute material to an audience larger than the group’s intended reading participants, or evade copyright or access controls.

An organizer who shares a book represents that they have the permission needed for that sharing. Rishi does not independently verify that representation before a share is created.

## 4. Session conduct

Reading Sessions are voice-first and audio-only. Participants must:

- Speak respectfully and allow others to participate.
- Follow the session’s speaker-floor and hold-to-talk controls.
- Respect a controller’s synchronized reading position and other participants’ ability to read independently.
- Avoid threats, targeted harassment, hate-based abuse, sexual exploitation, and unlawful conduct.
- Not record, rebroadcast, or externally distribute another participant’s voice or identity without the permissions required by law.

Video, recording, text chat, reactions, and persistent group posts are not part of the first release.

## 5. Age protections

Rishi may allow minors to participate using age bands, parental consent where required, private-by-default settings, and separation of adult and minor Reading Sessions. Users must provide accurate age information when requested and must not misrepresent their age to enter a restricted group or session.

The final age policy, consent mechanism, data handling, and jurisdiction-specific restrictions require legal review before launch.

## 6. Book copies and personal libraries

Joining a Reading Session automatically saves the shared book to the member’s Rishi library. Leaving the group or session does not delete that personal library copy. Group access and session links may end separately from a user’s personal library state.

Rishi does not control copies that users keep on their devices or in external services. Users remain responsible for how they use copies outside Rishi.

Rishi does not bypass DRM. Users must not upload or share material in a way that circumvents DRM, access controls, or license restrictions.

## 7. Copyright reports

Copyright reports are submitted through the official Rishi website. The app may link to that page but does not collect or adjudicate copyright claims.

A report should identify:

- The work claimed to be protected.
- The Rishi-hosted material, link, group, session, title/edition, or other locator.
- The reporter’s verified contact information.
- Evidence of ownership or authority to act.
- The reporter’s required accuracy and good-faith statements.

The reporter does not need to identify the uploader if Rishi can locate the hosted material from the information supplied. Rishi’s first-release policy accepts reports from verified corporate rights owners or authorized corporate representatives, subject to counsel’s review of whether additional legitimate rights holders must be accepted.

Rishi reviews reports before disabling access. Clearly credible reports may receive expedited handling. A report alone does not automatically remove a book.

## 8. Validated claims and exact hashes

When Rishi validates a claim, it may:

- Disable the affected Rishi-hosted copy and revoke its links after the active session’s natural end.
- Prevent new sessions from using that work.
- Globally block the exact SHA-256 hash across Rishi-hosted copies.
- Remove or disable exact matching hosted copies after the current session ends.

An exact hash is only a locator for identical bytes. It is not, by itself, proof that a work is infringing or that a reporter owns it. Modified or reformatted files are not automatically removed and require manual review.

Rishi does not remove personal or external copies that it does not host.

## 9. Appeals

An uploader may appeal through the same official website without creating a Rishi account. The appeal should include evidence of permission, a license, or another basis showing that the Rishi-hosted copy should not have been disabled.

The exact hash block remains active during review to prevent immediate re-uploading. If the appeal succeeds, Rishi may remove the block and restore affected Rishi-hosted copies where technically and legally appropriate.

Rishi does not automatically issue account warnings, strikes, or account penalties solely because of a validated copyright claim.

## 10. Reports, evidence, and privacy

Rishi restricts report and evidence access to the review team. Evidence is retained only while the report or appeal is active, plus a short legally required record period, and is then securely deleted unless a longer period is required by law or necessary to preserve a legal record.

Rishi may retain limited operational metadata, such as report status, hash block status, link revocation, and decision timestamps, for security, auditing, and legal compliance.

## 11. Enforcement of these rules

Rishi may remove a member from a group or session, revoke a session link, disable a hosted copy, or restrict a feature when necessary to enforce these rules, protect users, respond to a validated legal claim, or maintain service security. The product does not promise that every harmful or unlawful use will be detected before it occurs.

## 12. Contact and legal review

The final website should publish a dedicated copyright-report route and contact method. The legal owner of the service should determine whether Rishi should register a formal designated agent in the United States and what additional notices, counter-notice process, records, or policies are required in other jurisdictions.
